Choosing an Agent

How to Choose a Real Estate Agent You Can Trust in Canada

By Tanya Nabein
REALTOR® · Real Estate Cash Rebate, a service of Relocation Services Group

For most Canadians, your home is the single biggest financial decision you’ll ever make, and if you’re retiring or downsizing it may be tied to your retirement security. That makes the agent you choose more important than almost any other part of the move. The good news: a little homework upfront, most of it free and quick, is the best protection you have. Here’s how to find an agent you can genuinely trust.

Why does choosing the right agent matter so much?

A skilled agent does far more than open doors. They price your home realistically, protect your interests at the negotiating table, and guide you calmly through the paperwork that can feel overwhelming. The wrong agent, by contrast, can cost you real money, waste your time, and add stress at a moment when you can least afford it. For retirees and downsizers especially, where the proceeds often fund the years ahead, getting this choice right is worth slowing down for.

How do I confirm an agent is licensed and in good standing?

Every province has a regulator with a free, public search you can use before you sign anything. In Ontario, check RECO’s register; in British Columbia, BCFSA’s “Find a Professional”; in Alberta, RECA ProCheck; in Quebec, the OACIQ register. These tools confirm the person is registered, show which brokerage they work under, and flag any recent discipline or conditions on their licence. It takes about two minutes, and it’s the single best safeguard you have.

What questions should I ask before hiring someone?

Ask how well they know your specific neighbourhood, how many homes they’ve closed in the past year, and what the average days on market and list-to-sell ratio look like for properties like yours. Ask whether they specialize in your situation, whether that’s downsizing, settling an estate, or buying a first home, and request references from recent clients you can actually call. The answers matter, but so does the manner: a good agent gives you honest, specific replies rather than vague reassurance.

What are the red flags to watch for?

Be cautious of vague answers about local data, pressure to sign quickly, or a listing price that sounds too good to be true (sometimes a tactic to win your business, not to sell your home). Watch for poor communication, reluctance to share references, or anyone who brushes off your questions as if they’re a nuisance. A trustworthy agent welcomes scrutiny, because they know it’s your money and your home on the line. If something feels off in those early conversations, trust that instinct and keep looking.

Once I hire an agent, what are they legally required to do for me?

When you become a client, your agent and their brokerage owe you a fiduciary duty: the legal obligation to act in your best interest, be honest with you, keep your information confidential, and disclose anything that materially affects your decision. In Ontario this is set out under TRESA, the Trust in Real Estate Services Act, and similar duties of loyalty and care apply across the provinces. It’s a meaningful protection worth understanding. Note that it begins once you’re formally a client, which is one more reason to choose carefully before you sign.

Can a referral service do this screening for me?

It can help. A vetted-referral service does much of the legwork for you: confirming licensing and standing, reviewing track records, and matching you with agents suited to your situation, so you start from a shortlist rather than a phone book. It doesn’t replace your own judgment, and no honest service can guarantee a particular result. What it can do is save you real time and add a layer of confidence, which counts for a lot when the stakes are this high.

The bottom line

Trust isn’t something you have to take on faith. Between a free regulator search, a handful of pointed questions, and an awareness of the usual red flags, you can tell a strong agent from a weak one before you commit. Do that homework, lean on a vetted referral if you’d like a head start, and you’ll head into your move with the right person in your corner and far greater peace of mind.
